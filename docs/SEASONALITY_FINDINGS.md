# Seasonality Analysis - Key Findings

## Overview

This analysis examines seasonal unemployment patterns in the 2019 baseline year and assesses how COVID-19 disrupted normal seasonal cycles in 2020. The analysis reveals moderate baseline seasonality (CV: 11.90%) that was completely overwhelmed by pandemic timing effects.

**Analysis Date:** September 11, 2026  
**Period Studied:** January 2019 - November 2020  
**Focus:** Seasonal patterns, year-over-year changes, quarterly trends

---

## Executive Summary

**Key Findings:**
1. **2019 Baseline:** Moderate seasonal variation (11.90% coefficient of variation)
2. **Seasonal Pattern:** Q2 lowest unemployment, Q3 highest
3. **COVID Disruption:** Seasonal patterns completely overwhelmed from March 2020
4. **Largest YoY Change:** April 2020 (+982% increase vs 2019)
5. **Smallest YoY Change:** January 2020 (+25.8% increase vs 2019)

## 7. Key Findings Summary

### 1. Baseline Seasonality (2019)

**Characteristics:**
- **Moderate seasonal variation:** CV = 11.90%
- **Seasonal range:** 1.26 pp (May low to August high)
- **Quarterly pattern:** Q2 lowest, Q3 highest
- **State variation:** 0.34 pp range in seasonal patterns

**Interpretation:**
- Some seasonal fluctuation present but not extreme
- Predictable annual cycle: spring low, late summer high
- Consistent across states with minor variations
- Both rural and urban follow similar patterns

### 2. Year-over-Year Changes (2020 vs 2019)

**Magnitude:**
- **Average change:** +11.29 pp
- **Range:** +0.80 pp (January) to +25.89 pp (April)
- **Peak disruption:** April 2020 (+982% vs April 2019)

**Pattern:**
- Early 2020 (Jan-Feb): Minimal change (+26-58%)
- March 2020: Sudden spike (+257%)
- April-May 2020: Extreme increase (+944-982%)
- June-Nov 2020: Gradual decline but still elevated (+174-660%)

**Conclusion:** COVID-19 impact completely dominated year-over-year comparisons, overwhelming any baseline seasonal effects.

### 3. COVID Disruption of Seasonality

**Before COVID (2019 Pattern):**
- Predictable seasonal cycle
- Q2 (Apr-Jun) lowest unemployment
- Q3 (Jul-Sep) highest unemployment
- Variation: ±0.5 pp from annual mean

**After COVID Emergence (2020 Pattern):**
- Normal seasonal patterns obliterated
- April-May 2020: Should be seasonal low, actually became peak
- Q2 2020: Expected lowest quarter, became highest
- Seasonal signals drowned out by pandemic shock

**Pattern Inversion:**
- April 2019: 2.64% (seasonal low) ✓
- April 2020: 28.53% (extreme peak) ✗
- **Complete inversion of expected seasonal pattern**

### 4. Recovery and Seasonality

**Recovery Phase (July-November 2020):**
- Unemployment declining month-over-month
- But still 174-409% above 2019 levels
- Unknown: Will seasonal patterns re-emerge post-recovery?
- Question: Long-term impact on seasonal cycles?

## Implications

### For Economic Analysis

1. **Seasonal Adjustment Challenges:**
   - Traditional seasonal adjustment methods invalid for 2020
   - COVID shock creates structural break in time series
   - Need alternative approaches for 2020 data normalization

2. **Baseline Establishment:**
   - 2019 provides reasonable pre-COVID seasonal baseline
   - Moderate seasonality (CV: 11.90%) suggests some seasonal sensitivity
   - Future analysis should reference 2019 patterns for comparison

3. **Forecast Complications:**
   - Normal seasonal forecasting models unusable for 2020
   - COVID timing unpredictable, overwhelms seasonal signals
   - Recovery trajectory uncertain, may not follow historical patterns

### For Labour Market Understanding

1. **Spring Employment:**
   - Pre-COVID: Spring (Apr-Jun) typically best employment quarter
   - Possible factors: Agricultural season, hiring cycles, weather
   - COVID: Completely disrupted this pattern in 2020

2. **Late Summer Pattern:**
   - Pre-COVID: August typically highest unemployment
   - Possible factors: Post-monsoon, fiscal year transitions
   - COVID: Pattern not observable in 2020 due to recovery phase

3. **Seasonal Drivers:**
   - Agricultural cycles (rural areas)
   - Fiscal/academic year cycles (urban areas)
   - Weather and mobility factors
   - Festival season impacts (not captured in monthly data)

### For Future Research

1. **Pattern Re-emergence:**
   - Will 2019 seasonal patterns return post-COVID?
   - Or has COVID permanently altered seasonal cycles?
   - Need multi-year post-COVID data to assess

2. **Sectoral Seasonality:**
   - Different sectors likely have different seasonal patterns
   - Agriculture vs services vs manufacturing
   - COVID impact may vary by sector's seasonal sensitivity

3. **Regional Variations:**
   - 6 states show similar 2019 patterns
   - National-level seasonal patterns may differ
   - Climate zones may have distinct seasonal cycles

## Technical Notes

**Coefficient of Variation (CV):**
- CV = (Standard Deviation / Mean) × 100%
- Measures relative variability
- CV < 10%: Low seasonality
- 10% < CV < 20%: Moderate seasonality
- CV > 20%: High seasonality

**Seasonal Range:**
- Difference between highest and lowest monthly unemployment
- Measures absolute seasonal variation
- 2019 overall: 1.26 pp

**Year-over-Year (YoY) Change:**
- Compares same month across consecutive years
- Accounts for seasonal effects when stable
- 2020 YoY dominated by COVID, not seasonality
